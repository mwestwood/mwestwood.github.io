## Strategy 1: Volume Subtraction — Find the Missing Piece

These problems show a **completed rectangular prism** (or give its dimensions) and one or more **known pieces**. You must identify which of the answer choices is the missing piece that completes the solid.

**Steps:**

1. **Calculate the total volume** of the target rectangular prism:
   ```
   Total volume = Length × Width × Height  (in unit cubes)
   ```

2. **Count the unit cubes in the given piece(s).**

3. **Find the missing piece's volume:**
   ```
   Missing piece volume = Total volume − Given piece volume
   ```

4. **Match the shape** — choose the answer option whose cube count equals the missing volume AND whose shape fits into the remaining space.

{: .highlight }
> **Key insight:** Volume alone narrows it down; the *shape* (how the cubes are arranged) closes the deal. Always verify that the chosen piece can actually slot into the gap without overlap.

---

## Strategy 2: Silhouette Matching and Elimination

For problems where several answer choices have the correct volume, use **visual elimination**:

1. **Identify the gap's silhouette** from the front, side, and top views.
2. **Eliminate** any answer choices that protrude in the wrong direction or miss filling a required section.
3. The remaining candidate is the answer.

```
Three views to check:
  Top view   → confirms the footprint (L × W layout)
  Front view → confirms the height profile
  Side view  → confirms depth profile
```

---

## Strategy 3: Layer-by-Layer Fitting

Break the rectangular prism into **horizontal layers** (each 1 unit tall). For each layer:

1. Note which unit squares are occupied by the given piece.
2. Shade the remaining squares — that is what the missing piece must cover in that layer.
3. Assemble the layer-by-layer shapes into the 3-D missing piece.
4. Match against the answer choices.

## Worked Examples

### Example A — 2×2×2 Cube, One Piece Given

Target: a 2×2×2 cube (8 unit cubes). Given piece: an L-shaped triomino on the bottom layer occupying 3 cubes (positions front-left, front-right, back-left).

```
Total = 8
Given piece = 3 cubes (bottom layer L-shape)
Missing = 8 − 3 = 5 cubes

Bottom layer gap: 1 cube (back-right corner)
Top layer: all 4 cubes

Missing piece = 1 cube below + 4 cubes above → a shape like a corner column with a flat top.
```

Look for the answer option with **5 cubes** in an L-column arrangement.

---

### Example B — 3×2×2 Block, One Piece Given

Target: 3×2×2 = 12 unit cubes. Given piece: a 2×2×1 flat slab (4 cubes) on the left side.

```
Total = 12
Given = 4
Missing = 8 cubes → a 3×2×2 minus a 1×2×2 slab = a 2×2×2 cube on the right
```

Answer: a **2×2×2 cube** (8 cubes).

---

### Example C — Two Pieces Given

Target: 4×2×2 = 16 cubes. Piece A = 6 cubes (a 3×2×1 slab), Piece B = 4 cubes (a 2×2×1 slab).

```
Total = 16
Given = 6 + 4 = 10
Missing = 6 cubes

The gap is on the right side: 3×2×1 footprint, 1 unit tall — matches a 3×2×1 slab of 6 cubes.
```

## Solving Approach for Each Problem in the Workbook

Since these problems rely on the exact 3-D diagrams in the workbook, use this checklist:

```
Step 1: Count cubes in target solid  →  Total volume T
Step 2: Count cubes in given piece   →  Given volume G
Step 3: Missing volume M = T − G
Step 4: Eliminate answer choices with wrong cube count
Step 5: Among remaining choices, check shape fits the gap
        (check all three views: top, front, side)
Step 6: Choose the one that slots in perfectly
```

{: .note }
> If two answer choices have the same cube count, focus on **orientation**. One may need to be flipped or rotated in a way that is impossible given the gap's shape.

---

## General Tips for Form Solid Figure Problems

1. **Volume first, shape second.** Volume subtraction eliminates most wrong answers immediately.
2. **Count carefully.** Hidden cubes in 3-D diagrams are easy to miss — use the height marks and layer indicators in the diagram.
3. **Three views.** If you are unsure, mentally check the front, top, and side views of both the gap and the candidate piece.
4. **Rotation is allowed; reflection may not be.** Check whether the piece can be physically rotated to fit — some pieces look similar but are mirror images that cannot be rotated into each other.
5. **Trust the volume.** If only one answer choice has the right cube count, that is almost certainly correct (the shape fitting is then automatic).
6. **Draw it.** For complex pieces, sketch each layer on paper with a grid — it takes 30 seconds and prevents errors.
