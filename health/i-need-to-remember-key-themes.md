# I Need to Remember — Key Themes
{: .fs-9 }

A practical set of reminders for protecting energy, attention, and long-term health.
{: .fs-5 .fw-300 }

---

## Why a “Remember List” Matters

When life gets busy, the first things we drop are usually the most foundational: sleep, movement, food quality, and reflection. A short, high-signal list helps bring attention back to what compounds over time.

{: .note }
> This is not about doing everything perfectly. It is about returning to core habits quickly and consistently.

---

## Key Theme 1: Protect the Basics First

If the basics are unstable, everything else feels harder.

- **Sleep before optimization** — consistent sleep/wake windows improve mood, focus, and recovery.
- **Food quality over food perfection** — prioritize whole foods, protein, hydration, and consistency.
- **Daily movement** — walking, light strength, and mobility create momentum even on low-energy days.

---

## Key Theme 2: Build for Consistency, Not Intensity

Health outcomes are mostly driven by repeatable actions, not occasional heroic effort.

- Choose routines you can do on stressful days.
- Use “minimum viable habits” (example: 10-minute walk, 5-minute stretch, 1 healthy meal anchor).
- Track streaks lightly; avoid all-or-nothing thinking.

{: .important }
> Small actions done daily beat ambitious plans done rarely.

---

## Key Theme 3: Reduce Friction in Advance

Willpower is unreliable. Environment design is reliable.

- Keep healthy defaults visible (water bottle, prepared meals, workout clothes ready).
- Remove common triggers (late-night scrolling, easy junk snacking, overpacked mornings).
- Set up cues (calendar reminders, bedtime alarms, “shutdown” checklist).

---

## Key Theme 4: Manage Stress as a Health Skill

Stress management is not optional; it is part of physical health.

- **Nervous system downshift:** breathing, short walks, sunlight, quiet breaks.
- **Cognitive unload:** journaling, simple planning, brain-dump lists.
- **Social regulation:** regular check-ins with trusted people.

When stress is high, lower the bar and keep the habit alive.

---

## Key Theme 5: Remember Identity Over Outcomes

Long-term change sticks when tied to identity.

- “I am someone who takes care of my body.”
- “I am someone who resets quickly after setbacks.”
- “I am someone who keeps promises to myself.”

Focus on becoming the type of person who practices these behaviors, not just on short-term results.

---

## A Weekly Reset Template

Use this once per week:

- [ ] What helped my energy most this week?
- [ ] What drained me most?
- [ ] Which one habit will I protect next week?
- [ ] What friction can I remove today?
- [ ] Who can support me this week?

---

## Bottom Line

If you only remember one thing: **return to the basics quickly**. Sleep, movement, nourishment, stress regulation, and consistency are the core levers that make everything else easier.
